SECTION 05 SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & NEURAL VISION MANUAL UNDER THE SURFACE

How Cullet discerns physical matter.

Training an artificial visual system for municipal solid waste presents a unique challenge: discarded items are rarely pristine. They arrive crushed, soiled, fractured, and cast in variable ambient lighting.

Rather than classifying directly into abstract municipal streams (“biodegradable” or “hazardous”), Cullet employs a two-tier hierarchical classification taxonomy. The neural backbone is trained to discern 12 concrete visual material substances, including corrugated cardboard, high-density polyethylene, and amber glass.

These granular material probabilities are subsequently aggregated through municipal routing matrices to determine the final disposal stream. This decouples visual feature recognition from local municipal policy, allowing sorting rules to be updated without costly model retraining.

“

By separating material identification from municipal policy, the system mirrors how human recycling facilities operate: first observe what something is made of; only then decide which bin receives it.

PLATE I NEURAL BACKBONE

MobileNetV2 Architecture

Lightweight inverted residual bottlenecks with linear depthwise separable convolutions, pretrained on ImageNet-1k visual features.

PLATE II TRAINING STRATEGY

Transfer Learning + Fine Tuning

Two-phase optimization: frozen backbone head initialization followed by selective unfreezing of the top 30 feature extraction layers on ~10,600 balanced images.

PLATE III CLASSIFICATION HIERARCHY

12 Materials → 4 Municipal Streams

Two-tier routing table: granular material softmax scores are aggregated into four broad municipal streams (Biodegradable, Recyclable, Hazardous, Non-biodegradable).

PLATE IV VALIDATION METRIC

~89.6% Accuracy Benchmark

Evaluated across a stratified held-out test split of real-world municipal solid waste photography, demonstrating high discrimination on glass, fiber, and metal.

PLATE V INFERENCE ENGINE

Local TensorFlow Execution

Runs directly on local CPU with a preloaded MobileNetV2 model. Sub-150ms single-tensor forward pass with zero external cloud dependencies.

PLATE VI EDGE CONSTRAINTS

224 × 224 px Tensor Input

Standardized bicubic resampling with MobileNetV2 normalizations ([-1, 1] range) and client-side offscreen canvas thumbnail generation for local auditing.
